Comprehensive Mathematical Guide: Percentage Calculations, Formulas & Finance

Derived from the Latin per centum (meaning "by the hundred"), percentages are a universal mathematical language for expressing ratios, proportions, financial returns, and relative changes. Whether computing commercial retail discounts, assessing corporate profit margins, determining compound investment growth, or evaluating academic exam scores, mastering percentage mathematics is indispensable. 1. Core Formulas & Mathematical Derivations

1. What is P% of X?

Result = (P / 100) × X

Used to calculate sales tax, tipping, interest payments, or discounts. Example: 18% GST on a ₹4,000 service = (18 / 100) × 4000 = ₹720.

2. X is What Percent of Y?

Percentage = (X / Y) × 100

Calculates proportions, such as exam performance or budget allocations. Example: 450 marks out of 500 = (450 / 500) × 100 = 90%.

3. Percentage Change (Increase / Decrease)

Δ% = [(Vnew - Vold) / Vold] × 100

Measures inflation, stock price changes, or revenue growth. A positive result indicates growth; a negative result represents decline.

4. Reverse Percentage (Original Price)

Voriginal = Vfinal / [1 ± (P / 100)]

Determines pre-tax price or initial cost before a discount was applied. If a discounted item costs $80 after a 20% sale: 80 / 0.8 = $100.

2. Essential Finance: Profit Margin vs. Markup Explained

In commerce, business owners frequently confuse Margin and Markup, which can lead to severe pricing mistakes:

  • Markup: The percentage added to the wholesale cost to reach the selling price.
    Markup % = [(Selling Price - Cost) / Cost] × 100. If an item costs ₹100 and sells for ₹150, markup is 50%.
  • Profit Margin: The percentage of the final selling price that constitutes profit.
    Margin % = [(Selling Price - Cost) / Selling Price] × 100. On the same item, margin is (50 / 150) × 100 = 33.33%.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why do two consecutive 10% discounts not equal a 20% discount?

Sequential discounts apply multiplicatively rather than additively. If a ₹1,000 item receives a 10% discount, its price becomes ₹900. An additional 10% discount is then calculated on ₹900 (₹90 reduction), resulting in a final price of ₹810—which equals an effective overall discount of 19%, not 20%.

What is the difference between a percentage and a percentage point?

A percentage reflects relative proportion, while a percentage point measures the arithmetic difference between two percentages. For instance, if a central bank increases interest rates from 4% to 5%, the rate increased by 1 percentage point, but the relative increase in borrowing costs is 25% ((5 - 4) / 4 = 0.25).

How do I calculate GST or VAT included in a total receipt?

To extract tax from a gross amount: Tax Amount = Gross Total - [Gross Total / (1 + (Tax Rate / 100))]. For example, in a ₹1,180 receipt with 18% GST: 1,180 / 1.18 = ₹1,000 base price, meaning exactly ₹180 was GST.

Can percentages be greater than 100%?

Yes. Any growth exceeding the baseline value results in a percentage greater than 100%. For example, if a company's revenue doubles from $1M to $2M, it achieved a 100% increase; if it triples to $3M, it achieved a 200% increase.
