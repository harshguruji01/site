2. Editorial Headlines & Title Casing Rules (APA, Chicago, MLA)

In digital publishing and journalistic writing, formatting headlines correctly is critical for editorial authority. Major style guides agree on core principles with subtle variations:

  • Capitalize First and Last Words: Regardless of part of speech, the first and last word in a title or subtitle must always begin with an uppercase letter.
  • Capitalize Major Parts of Speech: Nouns, pronouns (including It, They), verbs (including linking verbs like Is, Are, Was), adjectives, and adverbs must be capitalized.
  • Lowercase Minor Words: Short prepositions (in, on, at, by, for, to), coordinating conjunctions (and, but, or, nor), and articles (a, an, the) remain in lowercase unless they appear at the start of a title or after a colon.

3. The Mechanics of Unicode & Case Folding

Converting text between cases is deceptively complex due to internationalization (i18n) nuances. In English ASCII, each lowercase letter maps 1:1 to an uppercase counterpart by altering bit 5 (difference of 32 in decimal value, e.g. 'a' (97) → 'A' (65)). However, under international Unicode standard (Unicode Technical Report #21), case conversions require Case Folding:

  • In German, the lowercase sharp s (ß) historically upper-cases to double S (SS).
  • In Turkish, a dotted lowercase i becomes an uppercase dotted İ, while a dotless ı maps to uppercase I.
  • Greek sigma has two lowercase forms (σ inside words and ς at word endings), which both map to uppercase Σ.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between Title Case and Sentence case?

In Sentence case, only the first letter of the first word and proper nouns (names of people, places, brands) are capitalized, mirroring natural prose. In Title Case, every major word (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) is capitalized, while articles and short prepositions remain lowercase unless they begin the heading.

Why are URL slugs always converted to lowercase kebab-case?

Web servers running on Linux filesystems treat URLs with case sensitivity (e.g., /Tools and /tools are distinct resources). Search engines like Google recommend lowercase words separated by hyphens (kebab-case) because hyphens act as explicit word separators in search algorithms, preventing duplicate content indexation penalties.

How does camelCase conversion handle punctuation and special characters?

The camelCase algorithm strips non-alphanumeric punctuation (hyphens, underscores, spaces) and capitalizes the immediate subsequent character while keeping the initial character lowercase, producing clean, valid software variable identifiers.
